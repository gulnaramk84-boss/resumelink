## What the work actually looks like

An AI Non-Profit Strategist sits between the mission and the tools. In a typical week you might map where the team loses hours (reporting, research, translation, scheduling), test one small improvement, write down the rules everyone follows, and explain the result to the director and the board in plain language.

The job is part strategist, part translator and part risk manager. You rarely build software. You decide **what is worth doing, what is safe, and how to prove it worked**.

## Where AI helps, and where it does not

- **Helps:** first drafts of reports and newsletters, summarising long documents, translating routine communication, organising interview notes, finding patterns in survey comments (with a human reading a sample).
- **Needs care:** funder research and prospect lists (verify each deadline and eligibility rule at the official source), anything with numbers, anything that reaches a donor or regulator.
- **Do not do:** feed personal data about beneficiaries into public tools, or let a tool decide who receives a service.

## The privacy line

Many organisations serve people in vulnerable situations. Treat their data as the most sensitive thing you hold. A safe default is: **aggregated or anonymised data only, no names, no contact details, no case notes**, and a named person who approves every new use.

## How to benchmark pay

Pay for this kind of role varies widely by country, funding model and organisation size, so published averages are unreliable. Instead: collect five current job postings in your market (Devex, ReliefWeb and local job boards list salary ranges for many roles), compare the stated ranges for similar titles, and, for the United States, check the [Bureau of Labor Statistics Occupational Outlook Handbook](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/) for related roles such as fundraising managers and program directors.
