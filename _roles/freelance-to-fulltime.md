## Two readers, one guide

This page is for freelancers who want to return to employment and for coaches who help them. The problems are the same: client work looks scattered on paper, interviewers worry about commitment, and money conversations get confusing because freelance income and salary are different things.

## Turn "projects" into a story

Employers hire people who can show a repeatable way of working. Pick a thread (a niche, a type of client, a type of problem) and arrange your projects around it. Three strong projects described with scope, your contribution and a result are better than twenty one-line entries.

## The money conversation

Freelance income includes costs that an employer covers or that disappear: unbilled time, taxes and social contributions, equipment, software, insurance and slow months. A fair comparison looks at **take-home pay and total benefits**, not at the headline number. Work out:

1. What you really took home in an average month last year.
2. What you would keep from a salary offer after tax, plus benefits such as health cover, paid leave, pension and equipment.
3. The lowest offer that beats your current situation, and the number you will actually ask for.

## What hiring managers quietly worry about

They rarely say it, but freelancers hear the same three doubts: independence (will you take direction?), loyalty (will you stay?) and teamwork. Answer them before they are asked, with stories rather than adjectives.
